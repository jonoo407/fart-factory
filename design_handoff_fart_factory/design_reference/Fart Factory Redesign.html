Design memo · Play-screen redesign

Make the one thing you should do
impossible to miss — and make the payoff a moment.

The core loop of The Fart Factory is genuinely good. The problem is staging: every system shouts at equal volume, so a new player (especially a 9-year-old) can't tell what to touch first, and the big reward — the crowd reacting — is a tiny line of text. Here's the re-staging, then three directions to react to.

Scope · whole app, screen by screen Device · phone-first Tone · gross-out & proud Audience · 9-yo accessible, adult-deep Stage · static mockups → prototype
01 What's going wrong today

One screen, fourteen jobs, no leader

The current play screen stacks the audience card, an eight-button toolbar (gold, notes, Kitchen Mode, Kitchen, Travel, Notebook, Shop, Mute), a daily quest, a belly meter, buffs, the plate, the location, the pantry, the launch button and the result — all at the same visual weight, top to bottom.

The problems

  • No focal point. The Launch button has the same pull as the mute toggle.
  • The goal is buried. What this crowd actually wants is one small italic line.
  • The payoff is text. The best moment in the game — the crowd reacting — barely registers.
  • Eight systems at once. Shop, Kitchen, Travel, Research, Notebook… all shown before a kid has launched a single fart.

The fix, in one sentence

Stage the screen as a three-beat rhythm with a single hero action per beat, blow the reaction up into a full-screen celebration, and reveal the meta-systems one at a time as the player earns them.

  • Read → Build → Blast → React. A clear verb at every step.
  • The crowd's craving becomes the headline, shown as icons a 9-yo can read.
  • The reaction takes over the screen — faces, stink cloud, a grade that slams down.
  • Progressive unlock (the code already has a feature-intro hook) keeps day one to four buttons.
02 The re-staged loop

Easy to learn on the surface, deep underneath

Same mechanics, restaged so the surface reads instantly and the mastery hides one layer down. The seven hidden axes (wet · dry · stink · loud · musical · length · temp) stay — kids match the big icon, adults tune the exact profile.

03 Three directions

Same loop, three personalities

Each is phone-first and gross-out. They differ in where the energy lives. Every direction is shown on the two screens that matter most: the play screen and the reaction payoff. Pick a lane (or mix) and I'll build it into a full clickable prototype, screen by screen.

04 Where this goes next

These are deliberately distinct so you can react. They also combine well: Order Ticket's clarity for day-one, Arcade's charge-meter + blast for the juice, Console's target-windows as an unlockable "expert view."

Tell me which direction (or hybrid) feels right, and what to push further — grossness, juice, depth.

STEP 2

I prototype

I build the chosen direction into a clickable phone prototype: working pantry → plate → charge → reaction, every screen.

STEP 3

We spec the new mechanics

Charge-timing combo, progressive unlock dock, target-window matching — written up so Claude Code can wire them into the real build.
