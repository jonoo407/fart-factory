01 Audio as a design system

The fart isn't a sound effect. It's the readout.

In most games audio is garnish. Here it's load-bearing: a fart is the output of your recipe, so it should be legible by ear — a wet brew squelches, a musical one plays notes, a hot one sizzles. Get that right and sound becomes the game's secret teacher (that's how Part 2 works). We build the whole mix from five layers — every clip pre-generated with ElevenLabs into a fixed sound bank, then chosen and layered at runtime. Nothing is generated live in the app.

1.2 The bank is finite

~200 clips cover the whole game

No live generation — so the trick is keeping the bank small. We quantise each axis into a few buckets and treat the fart as layers: one base rip (chosen by wet/dry + length bucket) plus optional stems (a melody if musical is high, a sizzle if hot, a longer haze tail if stinky), with loudness as a master gain. 02 Ingredient discovery & progression

Don't show the numbers. Make them earn the knowledge.

Right now every food wears its stats on its sleeve, so "matching" is just addition — solved, not played. The fix: hide a food's properties and let players discover them by launching it and reading the result (the sound from Part 1, and the crowd's face). Your pantry becomes a field guide that fills itself in automatically as you play — no menus, nothing to manage — and foods you use a lot get better. Easy to start (just try stuff, watch faces); deep to master (full knowledge + optimisation).

Cravings are qualitative too ("nothing too loud," "make it rotten"), so you read vibes, not target integers.

03 Recipe bonuses worth chasing

A recipe should be more than the sum of its smells.

If a named recipe just outputs the totals of its ingredients, there's no reason to care that it's named. So named recipes become combos that fire an emergent effect — a multiplier, an axis transform, a signature sound, a crowd bonus — that you can't get by adding numbers. Now the plate is closer to deckbuilding: you assemble known recipes and improvise, hunting synergies. And because every crowd wants something different, there's no single "best" recipe — you want a whole stable of them.

04 Progression: The Tour

Make every crowd a rung on a ladder you can see.

Hopping audience-to-audience felt flat because nothing framed the climb. The fix is three nested loops plus one new screen. Crowds gain rarity & difficulty; each location becomes a ladder of shows you watch yourself climb; and every show hands you something new — a food, an axis, a recipe. A 9-year-old feels a Candy-Crush clear; an adult feels a roguelite run.

4.3 Stakes, feedback & the pass gate

You have to earn the win — and you can see why

Playtest fix: the early build let you pass by adding random foods, with no feedback and no failure. Three rules close that gap (all live in the prototype now):

🎯 Matching has teeth

Being off-target hurts on a curve, the weakest judged axis counts for 45% of the score (you can't coast by nailing one), and violating a "hates" rule cuts up to 65%. Random plating now flops.

🧾 The judge card

Every reaction shows each judged axis with a ✓ / ~ / ✗, the crowd's target marker, and your actual level. The player learns exactly what to fix — no bare percentage.

🚦 Pass to advance

Under 50% = a flop: the only option is ↻ Try this crowd again — no advancing. Pass and you choose ↻ Improve (beat your score) or Next show ▶. Stars (1–3) gate the harder nodes.

No grind, no dead-ends. Gold pays only the improvement over your best on a crowd (no farming), and because more of a wanted axis always helps, a stuck player is one "add another" away from passing — the failure screen literally points at the ✗ axis. Exact formulas & constants live in the handoff package.

05 The flywheel

Each system feeds the next

🔊 Audio → Discovery

Because the fart sounds like its recipe, your ears teach you what ingredients do — no stat sheet needed.

🧪 Discovery → Recipes

Learning ingredients makes you experiment, and experiments stumble into named recipes worth keeping.

⚡ Recipes → Audio

Each recipe has a signature sound, so nailing one is an audio-visual payoff that pulls you back to Part 1.

5.1 Build notes

🎙️ ElevenLabs asset list

  • Fart SFX kit — wet/dry base rips, a pitched "toot" scale for musical, sizzle + haze tails, short→long takes. All pre-baked; the app only selects + layers them.
  • Crowd beds + reaction stingers — murmur loops per region; gasp / clap / laugh / retch / ovation / evacuate.
  • Character VO — host + each crowd: 1 craving line, 3 reaction lines (love / meh / evacuate). Distinct voices, <2s each.
  • Foley & UI — plops, thunks, charge whoosh, sweet-spot ding, coins, unlock fanfare, recipe-activate chord.

⚙️ For Claude Code
